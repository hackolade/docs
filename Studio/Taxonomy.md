# Taxonomy

**"How are business concepts classified and organized?"**

A **taxonomy** is a foundational component of semantic modeling and ontology design. It organizes business concepts into a structured classification system, typically through hierarchical relationships, progressing from general categories to more specific ones. It establishes a common vocabulary and provides a consistent way to categorize, organize, and navigate enterprise information.

While a taxonomy primarily focuses on classification and hierarchical relationships, an ontology goes further by defining properties, multiple types of semantic relationships, constraints, and logical axioms. A taxonomy can therefore exist independently or be incorporated into a richer ontology. It facilitates data discovery, classification, search, governance, and consistent interpretation of information by both humans and AI agents.

## Taxonomies with Hackolade Studio

Hackolade Studio's technology-independent Polyglot modeling capabilities already provide building blocks for defining taxonomies through [supertype/subtype hierarchies](Inheritancestrategies.md), multiple specialization axes, and completeness and exclusivity rules. Its [business glossary](Glossariesofbusinessterms.md) capabilities complement this by establishing standardized business terminology and definitions.

The addition of RDFS, OWL, and SHACL capabilities extends this foundation toward formal ontology representation and machine-interpretable constraints.

From a strategic positioning perspective, **taxonomies, semantic models, and ontologies are complementary forms of semantic metadata**, all of which can be designed, maintained, governed, and transformed within a unified modeling environment, then operationalized through semantic layers and knowledge graphs.
